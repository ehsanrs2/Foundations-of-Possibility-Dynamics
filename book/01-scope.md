# Chapter 1 — The problem of changing possibilities

**Status: conceptual synthesis and research proposal; not a discovery claim.**

## 1.1 What is a possibility?

The word *possible* refers to different mathematical and philosophical notions. A state may be consistent with physical laws yet unattainable to a particular agent with limited time and resources. A scenario may be reachable but unlikely, valuable but infeasible, or simply unknown to an observer.

We therefore distinguish:

1. **Physical admissibility:** not excluded by the chosen underlying physical theory.
2. **Operational reachability:** accessible from a specified state using explicitly allowed transformations, constraints, horizon and resources.
3. **Epistemic possibility:** entertained by an observer given limited knowledge.
4. **Desirability:** an external normative evaluation, not implied by reachability.

The present book studies **operational** possibility in bounded models. It neither measures the set of all physical futures nor establishes that new physical laws come into existence during innovation.

## 1.2 Generating and losing capabilities

Acquiring a capability can create new actions, and those actions can change the set of reachable outcomes. Losing a shared component can close several previously accessible paths. Combining tools may restore an interrupted service without making any *new end goal* reachable before the disruption.

The core research question is:

> Under matched initial capabilities and resource constraints, does the structure of *constructible future capabilities* carry independent information about performance after disturbances?

The word *independent* is essential. Network robustness, control theory, viability theory, information-theoretic empowerment, and innovation models already address adjacent parts of this problem [Aubin; Tria et al.; Klyubin et al.].

## 1.3 What would count as progress?

A useful result would show a rigorously defined metric, a clear operational interpretation, a reproducible predictive improvement over strong baselines, sensitivity analyses, and at least one credible limit or failure case. Progress could equally be a negative result identifying conditions under which generative capability structure adds nothing.

## 1.4 What this work does not claim

This edition does **not** establish a new science, a universal measure of future possibility, a novel theorem about open-ended evolution, or a verified predictive effect. Our toy case checks only internal intelligibility.

## 1.5 Reader's roadmap

Chapters 2–5 map existing literature, specify a finite-system formalism, define candidate measures, and explain a deliberately small example. The research protocol describes the measurements necessary before treating the conjecture as supported.


## 1.6 Critical review: claim narrowed to bounded feature utility

[The reviewer response](../research/novelty-audit-v0.3.md) distinguishes a mere formal re-encoding, which **cannot** establish a new scientific object, from an actual measured finite-computation or finite-data benefit. An exactly computed feature cannot contain information absent from the complete source representation already available to an unrestricted model. Any defendable future contribution is presently a **conditional candidate feature-engineering or computational-compression result**, with explicit equal-information baselines and falsifiers. None has been demonstrated.


## 1.7 Why consider this research program at all?

The motivating problem is how a system's **operational ability to construct, preserve and rebuild future capabilities** changes with resources, constraints and faults. This is a coherent question but **does not automatically require a new science**: viability, state-space reachability, fault trees and agent empowerment already address related objects. The program is justified as a *testable comparison*, not a presupposed discovery. The [mission and falsifiability statement](../research/scientific-motivation-and-boundaries.md) enumerates the core questions, ordinary methods that can already answer them and what kind of measurable advantage would count as useful progress.
