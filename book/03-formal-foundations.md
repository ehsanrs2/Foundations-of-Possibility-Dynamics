# Chapter 3 — A finite mathematical model

**Status: candidate definitions. Every object below is a modeling choice.**

## 3.1 State and capabilities

Let \(U\) be a finite universe of representable capability types. Let \(C_t\subseteq U\) be the capabilities actually available at time \(t\); let \(r_t\in\mathbb{R}_+^d\) denote consumable resources and \(e_t\) environmental conditions. The system state is

\[
s_t=(C_t,r_t,e_t).
\]

The horizon \(H\), initial budget \(B\), and admissible operations must be stated explicitly. We **do not** assume the finite model resolves truly unforeseen types outside \(U\). A later extension may enlarge \(U\) along a modeled generative grammar, but that is still a specified mathematical possibility space.

## 3.2 Production rules

A capability-creation rule \(\rho\) has prerequisites \(P_\rho\subseteq U\), an output \(o_\rho\in U\), cost \(k_\rho\ge 0\), latency \(\tau_\rho>0\), and environmental precondition \(q_\rho(e)\). The rule is enabled if \(P_\rho\subseteq C_t\), \(q_\rho(e_t)\) holds, and required resources exist.

Define \(\mathcal A(s_t)\) as the available operations (including construction, use and optional deletion). A legal transition is

\[
s_{t+1}\in T(s_t,a_t,\omega_t),\qquad a_t\in\mathcal A(s_t),
\]

where \(\omega_t\) may represent a failure. For stochastic settings, replace the relation by an explicitly specified transition kernel.

## 3.3 Reachable outcomes

Given target predicates \(g\in\mathcal G\) and legal policies \(\pi\),

\[
\mathcal R_{H,B}(s)=\{g\in\mathcal G:\exists\pi,\exists t\le H\text{ such that }g(s_t)=1,
\text{ with total cost}\le B\}.
\]

This is **existential, deterministic** reachability unless a stochastic threshold is added. Do not interpret it as a probability or utility. For comparison under disturbances, condition both the initial state and feasible policies on the same intervention distribution.

## 3.4 Construction closure

Define \(\operatorname{Cl}_{H,B}(C;r,e)\) as the set of capabilities obtainable by legal sequences of rule applications within horizon and resource constraints. Simply computing unrestricted graph transitive closure is insufficient: AND-prerequisites, consumed inputs, timing and mutually exclusive resource allocations matter.

## 3.5 Identifiability and model validity

Two descriptions may encode identical operational transitions despite different named capabilities. A metric that changes under harmless relabeling is suspect. More strongly, a metric could exploit hidden simulator rules unavailable to a real decision-maker. We must state observation assumptions, computational cost and what is measurable before each disturbance.

## 3.6 Limits

This construction formalizes **bounded reachable possibility** only. It does not answer whether future concepts can be described before they emerge, nor does it imply that physical possibilities are literally generated by the model.
