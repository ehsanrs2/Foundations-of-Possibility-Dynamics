# Chapter 2 — Relationship to existing research

**Status: selective literature map, not yet a systematic review.**

## 2.1 Reachability and viability

Reachable sets and viability kernels are central to control and viability theory. A viability kernel collects initial states from which admissible dynamics can remain inside constraints. These concepts already articulate the difference between an unconstrained possibility and a practically available trajectory. See Aubin's *Viability Theory* (original 1991 publication; 2009 reprint).

**Research gap to investigate, not asserted:** whether an explicit representation of endogenously constructible *action types* adds predictive content in selected bounded settings.

## 2.2 Adjacent possible and novelty

Tria, Loreto, Servedio and Strogatz (2014) describe a growing space of correlated novelties and empirically investigate novelty statistics. This work is essential prior art; claims about “first modeling newly appearing possibilities” would be incorrect.

Our proposed emphasis differs: measurable backup paths and future capability construction under adverse interventions, rather than novelty accumulation alone.

## 2.3 Recent higher-order novelty and quantitative synthesis

Di Bona and colleagues (2025) model **higher-order novelties**, so mathematical formation of new combinations is established prior art, not a uniquely new feature of the present proposal. More significantly, Loreto, Servedio and Tria published *The Science of the New* (Oxford University Press) in February 2026. It supplies a cross-domain quantitative synthesis of innovation. These sources strengthen the caution against advertising Possibility Dynamics as the first general science of new possibilities.

A distinct emphasis on reconstructing post-failure function may still be useful, but requires a mathematical comparison with existing repair and network theory. Luo and colleagues (2025), for example, analyze failure-recovery on interdependent hypergraphs; this is related work, not yet shown to be equivalent to the GBR candidate.

## 2.4 Empowerment and agency

Klyubin, Polani and Nehaniv (2005) define empowerment using information-theoretic capacity of an agent's action-to-perception channel. The measure connects control over future outcomes to the structure of actions. It is not equivalent to a simple count of nodes or reachable goals.

A fair benchmark must consider empowerment or an explicitly justified feasible approximation; it cannot be treated as if there were no pre-existing future-option measures.

## 2.5 Resilience and network redundancy

Multiple paths and robustness to failures are standard network-science concerns. Constructible backup routes may be representable as reachability in an augmented state-transition graph, and thus may be a **reparameterization** of an existing idea. This is a key threat to novelty.

## 2.6 Required novelty audit

Before publishing a novelty claim, conduct targeted searches for **dynamic hypergraphs**, **AND/OR dependency networks**, **network repair and self-healing**, **temporal reachability**, **evolvability**, **real options**, and **reconfigurable systems**. Document inclusion criteria and compare mathematical definitions rather than their names alone.

The operational objective is to test an incremental predictive contribution, not to win a terminology dispute. The working bibliography records only verified citations and unresolved retrieval work.


## 2.7 Fault trees and minimal cut sets

Ruijters and Stoelinga (2015) review conventional, dynamic and repairable **fault-tree analysis**, including path sets, cut sets, common-cause dependencies and probability-of-failure models (DOI: [10.1016/j.cosrev.2015.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cosrev.2015.03.001)). Their work is a substantive prior comparator for the binary independence predicate in GBR. A pair of supports with disjoint **declared exposed tokens** is not automatically a new concept of fault tolerance or a calibrated probability.

The [narrow correspondence table](../research/novelty-audit-v0.3.md) now separates reachability, dynamic AND/OR networks, fault trees, repair, viability, and empowerment. It is a **work-in-progress map, not a completed systematic novelty audit**.
