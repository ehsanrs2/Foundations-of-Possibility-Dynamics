# Chapter 10 — A provisional synthesis

**Status: conclusion of a working scientific monograph, not an experimental result.**

Our starting question asks how systems may acquire, lose, preserve and rebuild practically reachable capabilities. A finite formalism allows construction rules to be represented alongside ordinary actions, resource budgets and deadlines.

The candidate GOG quantity counts newly accessible end goals. The candidate GBR gain instead counts end goals acquiring a first pair of independently vulnerable plans under a chosen shock model. The existing toy scenario illustrates why these differ: a second route may help recover the original goal without creating any new goal.

The central limitations are substantive. Shared dependencies may destroy both paths. Plan feasibility is conditional on time, costs and available components. Existing state-space and network methods may represent exactly the same mechanism. The toy example has been reproduced as software but no ensemble study has yet established incremental predictive value.

Consequently, **neither a general physical law nor a separate scientific discipline has been demonstrated**. The research program should advance through preregistered counterexample-driven comparisons with strong baselines, and remain prepared to publish a negative result. The value of this edition lies in making its scientific commitments auditable.


## Reviewer verdict and release limitation (v0.3 audit)

The critical review of Issues #8 and #9 provides two additional reasons to retain **Major Revision** status. (i) Given the complete input system model, an exact deterministic GBR feature is informationally redundant for an unconstrained optimal predictor; potential gains must come from restricted representation/computation or additional input, properly accounted for. (ii) Raw binary GBR can count two labels for **one physical backup mechanism** as two independent exposed plans. Distinctly named implementation rules are not evidence of separate independent fault units.

[The novelty audit](../research/novelty-audit-v0.3.md) and [reproducible negative controls](../research/gbr-metric-audit-v0.3.md) narrow rather than settle the theory's scientific claims. **No hypothesis test has established prediction or causation, and Issues #3 and #8–#12 remain open.** Negative results should remain visible in every subsequent edition.

## R1 restriction on scientific claims

[Appendix C](../appendices/C-fault-event-ontology.md) and its small executable negative controls show how to require declared physical fault-event semantics **before** scoring route independence. This is a quality gate and a familiar reliability-modeling principle, not a novel GBR result or a validated ontology. [Appendix D](../appendices/D-predictive-estimands.md) lays out three distinct inferential questions and a tentative risk-set endpoint for future tests. Both appendices reduce ambiguity but leave H1, reviewer Issue #9 and the critical predictive scientific program **OPEN**.


## Why a useful answer need not be a new law

The research program investigates changing *operational* options, not newly appearing physical laws. The [explicit research motivation](../research/scientific-motivation-and-boundaries.md) distinguishes an important question from the unproven necessity of a distinct science. [R6](../research/R6-variable-risk-and-exact-baseline.md) adds a standard exact full-model reference, making the central novelty test harder in an appropriate way: if the model already contains every needed observable and unrestricted computation is available, an extra deterministic GBR feature cannot improve optimal prediction. Empirical value, if any, must be shown under fixed data/capacity/compute limits with real or independently validated outcomes.
