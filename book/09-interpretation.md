# Chapter 9 — The ontological and epistemic boundaries

**Status: philosophical interpretation; distinct from empirical findings.**

A state that is not forbidden by basic physics is not necessarily reachable from current conditions. A future that an observer has never imagined is not automatically physically new. A resource-bounded actor's feasible actions define yet another meaning of possibility.

In this book, operational possibility is indexed to the chosen rules, initial state, cost budget and time horizon. It must never be silently equated with the universe of physically possible events.

Novel combinations can be novel **relative to a history** while remaining representable in the initial model's grammar. Claims about ontically new possibilities would require a separate testable distinction, rather than merely a larger list of constructed capabilities.

More options also do not imply better welfare. Reachable harmful states and dangerous capabilities are still options. Utility, ethics and safety constraints must be introduced explicitly.

A correlation between a pre-shock measure and a later recovery outcome does not demonstrate that interventions which increase that measure cause improved resilience. Causal claims require intervention designs and assumptions.

Scientific status must be earned through transparent boundaries, comparative results and independent replication, rather than by declaring a compelling label to be a new academic field.
