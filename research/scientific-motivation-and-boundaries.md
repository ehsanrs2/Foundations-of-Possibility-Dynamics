# Why study Possibility Dynamics? — motivation and the novelty boundary

**Status:** research problem statement, not the discovery of a new physical law or an independently validated scientific discipline.

## The proposed subject

The book asks whether a system's **ability to create, preserve, lose and rebuild operationally reachable future capabilities** yields useful representations for predicting behavior after disturbances. The object is **bounded operational possibility**: given available capabilities C, consumable resources r, environment e, legal actions, time H and cost B, which targets are reachable and how can the reachable set change when executable construction operations alter future capabilities?

This is NOT the set of all physically possible futures and NOT epistemic uncertainty or a desirability/utility score. Constraints and information available to the operator must be explicit.

## Questions worth asking

| Specific research question | Required measurement |
|---|---|
| What constructive operations make a new capability reachable? | AND prerequisites, costs, time, available resources and admissible actions |
| Do new capabilities lead to new goals or just additional routes to existing goals? | GOG vs explicitly scoped route/backup measures |
| Which common-cause events or resource bottlenecks close those routes? | Physical mechanism identity, basic fault events, cut sets, resource loss |
| Can multiple individually feasible backup routes be prepared under one non-clairvoyant policy? | Actual pre-fault preparation, shared inventory and restoration on one history |
| Does a pre-failure structural feature predict *actual strict restoration after loss*? | A frozen policy, at-risk outcome, independent held-out families, calibrated uncertainty |
| Are the representation and results better than existing methods? | Equal-information viability/control/augmented-state/fault-tree/path-set/empowerment baselines with computation counted |

A simple irrigation/pumping example can motivate the question: two plants with identical current delivery may have different spare parts, tools and time-to-assemble alternate channels. That difference could matter after a pump failure; equally, a single shared power supply or inadequate assembly time can make the apparent alternative useless. The example is illustrative and NOT field evidence.

## The crucial comparison with prior knowledge

Viability and reachability already characterize legal future trajectories under constraints; finite capability creation is expressible as transitions in an augmented state model. Fault-tree/path/cut methods already describe dependent failure risks. Empowerment already addresses future control using information theory. These are substantive antecedents, not straw methods. The book cannot claim to be the first science of future possibilities.

The narrowed *testable* hypothesis is that an explicitly constructive, physically scoped pre-fault representation may improve **finite-data / finite-capacity / fixed-compute** prediction of non-clairvoyant strict restoration, after granting baselines exactly the same raw model and feature-computation resources.

If Z=GBR(X) is deterministic from a complete pre-event model X, the unrestricted optimal predictor with X gains no new information from Z. Thus any observed benefit would be a *representation, complexity or practical finite-sample benefit*, not new physical information. Negative results or representation failures are valid scientific outcomes and must remain in the book.

## What would establish a genuine need?

At least one prespecified, independently reproducible advantage over serious existing baselines, with meaningful effect size, uncertainty, robust fault identity and representation invariance, realistic test populations and honest accounting of time/memory. If the hypothesis fails under this comparison, the framework may be a useful explanatory vocabulary or domain-specific feature engineering rather than an independent theory. This is not an embarrassment; it answers the central question.
